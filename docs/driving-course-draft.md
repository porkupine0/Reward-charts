# Eli's driving course: draft research and video list

Status: **brainstorm draft, nothing built yet.** Made Nov 18, 2026.

## What the research says (from search summaries; most sources block automated reading, so check before relying on numbers)
- **Strongest for a video course: hazard-anticipation training** ("what's the hidden danger?", pause-and-predict). It reliably improves hazard spotting in tests (review of 19 studies, PMC4483194). One NHTSA-funded trial (RAPT) found about 24% fewer crashes for young men and no clear effect for young women. Gains fade without refreshers.
- **Knowledge-only and classroom driver's ed have not been shown to cut crashes** (Cochrane review of 3 trials with about 18,000 students; DeKalb study). They mostly led to earlier licensing.
- **Retention: short quizzes, spaced over weeks and mixed across topics,** beat rereading (Dunlosky 2013). Not tested in driver education specifically.
- **Commentary driving** (saying hazards out loud, for example as a passenger while a parent drives) brought young drivers' hazard spotting up to experienced levels in a lab study. No crash data.
- **Avoid crash/shock videos and car-control "bravado":** they didn't reduce risk-taking and may increase it.
- **What lowers teen crash risk later:** graduated licensing limits, lots of varied supervised driving, and parent-set rules on passengers, night driving and phones. A parent-teen agreement led to fewer violations but no proven crash drop.
- **Bottom line:** videos and quizzes build road knowledge and hazard-spotting. They don't teach car control or real-traffic judgement, and aren't a reason to license early.
