# Eli's driving course: 11-week plan (draft, Nov 18, 2026)

**Goal (permit test in about 3 months, mid-February 2027):** pass Iowa's written permit test (practice test: 25 questions, 80% to pass; the real test's length and pass mark aren't confirmed by an official page) and know the road before he drives.

## Shape of the course
- **10 units of 5 lesson days each, then a final week: 55 lesson days** (days 1–4: a video lesson with a 5-question quiz; **day 5: the unit test**, 16 questions: 4 from each of the unit's lessons, never-asked ones first, then ones he missed; scored, shown on the Parents page). Lessons run Monday to Friday, one a day. From Nov 19 to Feb 18 there are 66 weekdays, so this leaves about 11 days of slack for Thanksgiving, Christmas, New Year's, sick days and chore-locked days.
- **Knowledge tests** (25 questions like the real test, same topic mix every time, scored per topic, no answers shown):
  - **Baseline** (Form A): now, before lesson 1. **Built.**
  - **Progress tests** every two units: each opens once the unit test of units 2, 4, 6 and 8 is finished (about every two weeks, usually a weekend).
  - **Final** at the end of the final week.
  - **A different test every time** (Forms A–F, decided Nov 18): all new questions, though a rule can come back in a new situation. Every form has the same topic mix and the same number of number questions (distances, speeds, limits), but scores on different forms can still differ by a question or two from form difficulty alone. The baseline asked signs in words; later forms show drawn sign shapes.
- **A check against Iowa DOT's own practice test after unit 5** (once, about halfway): if his score there is well below the app's, there's time to change course. It's also the only place he sees picture and diagram questions before the final week.
- **Backing and reverse parking get their own day** (unit 8, day 3), since backing into every space is a family rule. The manual's backing section (4.7) gives the quiz: turn your head, don't rely on mirrors or the camera, go slow, turn the wheel the way you want the car to go. Backing *into a space* isn't in the manual, so the steps and the family rule go on that day's Key points card, labelled as the family's rule, not quizzed as law.
- **Iowa's permit and license rules** (who can supervise, no phones, the 12:30–5 a.m. limit, passengers) aren't in the manual; their questions quote Iowa DOT's pages, saved in docs/iowa/.
- **Weekly hazard ride** (family said yes): once a week, a 10-minute ride with Eli in the passenger seat calling out hazards ("car backing out on the left", "kid near the curb", "brake lights ahead"); a parent ticks it on the Driving page. Not part of the written test; it builds the skill the research rates highest.
- **Taking the test at home** (the family's plan): a parent applies to proctor the knowledge test online (Iowa DOT's Application to Proctor Knowledge Exam form). Processing takes 7–14 days, the emailed link expires after 72 hours, and he needs a computer (not a phone or tablet). After passing, book a DMV or county treasurer appointment to get the permit issued. **Apply about two weeks before the final week** (late January), once his Iowa DOT practice-test scores are at 80% or better.
- **Final week:** two Iowa practice-question videos and Iowa DOT's own online practice test (three passes at 80% or better before he books the real one); every day still ends with 5 in-app questions, his missed ones first; the final test on day 5.
- **Reading his scores:** guessing alone gets about 6 of 25 right; a change of 2 or 3 questions between sittings is noise, not progress; Iowa DOT's practice test has 3 choices per question (ours have 4), so its scores run a little higher.
- **Questions:** written from the Iowa manual (every answer quoted from its page and checked by a test), on the video's topic and on what Iowa's practice test asks. I can't watch the videos (YouTube blocks automated reading), so **your preview is the check that the video matches its questions.** A "Key points" card above each video gives the manual's facts in a few lines, so a half-watched video still teaches the test material.
- **Topic weights** come from Iowa DOT's 68 practice questions: general rules (speed, turns, passing, lights) 34%, signs 15%, school buses (10%) and emergency vehicles 15%, highways/weather/road types 15%, signals and markings 7%, railroad and work zones 6%, right of way 4%, alcohol and phones 4%.

## Skill levels (tagged Nov 21, 2026)
Each lesson day is tagged in `LEVELS` in scripts/build-video-plan.py; `python3 scripts/build-video-plan.py --levels`
checks every day has a tag and that no advanced day comes before what it relies on, and prints these counts.

- **Beginner:** Eli knows and can recall a rule, sign, colour, shape or number (what a pennant means; 3 seconds
  behind). Most of the permit test.
- **Intermediate:** he applies the rules to a situation that needs one decision (who goes first; when a lane change
  is safe; what to do for a school bus from the front).
- **Advanced:** he combines several rules under pressure, in rare or risky conditions, or needs car control:
  freeway merges, passing, skids and hydroplaning, night and snow, gravel and deer, brake failure, the diverging
  diamond, and **backing and reverse parking** (unit 8 day 3; the family rule: he backs into every space). The app
  teaches what to know and look for; the car skill itself is practised on the weekly hazard ride.
- Unit 11 (final week) is review: practice tests across all levels.

## Likely test questions
Each of Iowa DOT's 68 practice questions maps to a unit: signs, signals and the sign pictures to unit 2; markings and lane changes to 3; right of way to 4; speed, stopping, turning, signaling distances and speed limits to 5; passing, freeway entrances and merging to 6; school buses (7 questions, the most of any one topic: their own day), emergency vehicles and Move Over (3) and motorcycles to 7; railroad, work zones, parking and backing to 8; headlights, rain, hydroplaning, gravel and skids to 9; phones, alcohol, implied consent and tires to 10; looking ahead, blind spots and following distance to 1.
**At least 7 have no text in the manual.** I searched the manual for these and found nothing: which way to steer in a skid, the first half-hour of rain being slickest, time being the only way to sober up, a missed freeway exit, avoiding panic stops, common crash causes, and left-turn lane position (the other stems weren't each searched). Every quiz answer must quote the manual, so the app won't ask these; the videos for those days touch some of them, and **Iowa DOT's online practice test covers them** (final week).
**Pictures:** Iowa DOT's practice test shows 17 pictures (checked Nov 18): **7 blank sign shapes** (stop octagon, yellow-green school pentagon, yield triangle, orange diamond, yellow diamond, yellow pennant, slow-moving-vehicle triangle), all drawn by the quiz's sign shapes (signs.js); **3 signs with symbols** (Do Not Enter, Merging Traffic, Keep Right) that blank shapes can't show, so unit 2 describes them in words; and **7 road diagrams** (4 school-bus scenes on two-, three- and four-lane roads, 2 intersections, 1 lane-arrow road) asked in words in units 3, 4 and 7.
